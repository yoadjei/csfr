This supplement holds the tables that support the main paper without carrying
one of its findings. Every table is generated from the released result files by
\texttt{scripts/render\_tables\_v2.py}, the generator of the main paper's tables,
and a test fails if a table here differs from what its source file gives.
Numbers prefixed with S refer to this document. Unless a caption says
otherwise, the corpus is GovDocs1, the primary corpus of the paper, and every
value is a mean over its patches and mask seeds.

and~\ref{tab:supp:ssim} give hole PSNR and hole SSIM at all five.

\section{What the comparison methods compute}
\label{sec:supp:baselines}

Two classical methods fill the hole with the observed mean when their own
procedure cannot run: dictionary learning when the patch has fewer fully observed
tiles than atoms, and exemplar inpainting when no fully observed exemplar exists
at its smallest size. Table~\ref{tab:supp:fallbacks} gives how often that
happened in every cell. Dictionary learning also runs at the settings of the
released baseline, whose cap on the coordinate-descent sparse coding inside the
dictionary fit is often reached; Table~\ref{tab:supp:dictcap} compares it with a
larger cap. The shift-map implementation is not a function of its input, and
Table~\ref{tab:supp:shiftmap} measures how much its output varies between runs.

\section{Stopping rule}
\label{sec:supp:calibration}

The gap tolerance of the solver was calibrated against reference solves on cells
of the DFRWS 2006 corpus. The criterion
compares each solve with the minimiser, not with the ground truth, so it fixes
numerical accuracy and cannot tune reconstruction quality.
Table~\ref{tab:supp:calibration} gives the full grid.
